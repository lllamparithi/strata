128.6 m Red beds Core 4 / 7

The red is rust. The rust is oxygen.

Iron in these grains met free oxygen and never recovered. The colour is a record of an atmosphere — the only surviving photograph of air that has not existed for a very long time.

Cross-bedding here dips consistently. Wind had a preferred direction, and it held it for centuries.

188.9 m Black shale Core 5 / 7

Where the light budget runs out.

Shale this dark means water that never mixed. Nothing on the floor to stir it, nothing alive to eat the carbon. The rock splits into sheets thinner than a fingernail and smells faintly of oil when it is freshly broken.

244.2 m Intrusive Core 6 / 7

Something hot came through sideways.

A sheet of magma cut the bedding at an angle and cooked twenty centimetres of rock on either side of itself. The veins that branch off it are the cooling cracks, filled later by whatever the water was carrying.

300.0 m Basement Core 7 / 7

Bottom of hole.

Below this the rock has been melted and re-formed enough times that it no longer remembers being anything else. There are no more layers. There is no more record. The core barrel comes up warm.
